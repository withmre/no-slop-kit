# English catalogue

## Punctuation and layout

- Em dash and en dash as punctuation (absolute). Use a comma, a period, parentheses, or rebuild the sentence. A colon only when it sets up what follows.
- Colon setups in series: "The result:", "Here's the thing:", "The kicker:".
- Title Case On Every Heading when the house style is sentence case.
- Decorative bold, one bolded phrase per sentence.
- Emoji (absolute unless asked), including as bullet markers.
- Bullets where a paragraph reads better. "**Term**: explanation" on every line.

## Constructions

- "It's not X, it's Y" (absolute) and variants: "not just X, but Y", "more than X, it's Y", "takes more than X", "X isn't about Y. It's about Z.", "less X, more Y", "X, not Y" as a punchline.
- "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- Rhetorical question answered right away: "The result? A 20% increase." "The catch?"
- Reflexive groups of three. Cut the weak item, merge, or keep four if each carries meaning. Turning every triplet into a pair is another tic.
- Staccato fragments for punch: "Simple. Fast. Reliable."
- Parataxis: three or more short declarative sentences in a row, each standing alone. Connect them with because, but, so, which, a subordinate clause or a semicolon. Semicolons are fine; good human writers use them.
- The hedging seesaw: a claim, its counterpoint at equal weight, then a vague middle. Take the position and give the counterpoint one sentence.
- One sentence per line with blank lines between (LinkedIn style).
- Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era of", "Imagine a world where", "Have you ever wondered", "Whether you're X or Y", "When it comes to", "At its core", "From X to Y" as a range opener.
- Paragraph starters: "Overall,", "Indeed,", "Importantly,", "In essence,".
- "As a [role], I...": just say the thing.
- Signposting: "In this article, we'll explore", "Let's dive in", "Here's why", "Let's break it down", "Without further ado", "Buckle up", "Here's the thing", "Here's the deal".
- "Here's where X comes in", "This is where it gets interesting", "But that's not all".
- Summary closers: "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day", "In summary", "In a nutshell", "The bottom line is".
- Inspirational closers and engagement bait: "The future belongs to those who", "What do you think? Let me know in the comments."

## Hedges and filler

- "It's worth noting", "It's important to note", "It's important to remember", "Notably", "Interestingly".
- "may potentially", "could possibly", "in some cases", "to some extent", "arguably".
- "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally" in series.
- Intensifiers: "truly", "really", "deeply", "incredibly", "absolutely", "genuinely", "honestly".

## Leftovers (absolute)

"Certainly!", "Absolutely!", "Sure!", "Great question", "I'd be happy to", "I hope this helps", "I hope this email finds you well", "Please don't hesitate to reach out", "As an AI", "Here's a revised version", placeholders in brackets (except the kit's `[À FOURNIR : ...]` marker, which stays and gets listed), Markdown symbols in a plain-text channel.

## Channels

- Email and DMs: no headings, no bold, the ask in the first two lines.
- Social posts: no "Thread:" or 🧵 opener, no one-sentence-per-line layout, zero to two hashtags woven in, no engagement bait.

## Rhythm and tone

- Uniform sentence and paragraph length.
- Nominalizations ("the implementation of the optimization of") instead of verbs.
- Passive voice when the actor is known.
- Forced enthusiasm, flattering the reader, superlatives without evidence.
- "do not", "cannot", "it is" everywhere in casual text: use contractions.
